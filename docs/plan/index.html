What the research says

Verdict: build it, but make it different

The genre sells

  • Nodebuster: about 400k copies at about $2, 97% positive, 4 hours, one big tree that unfolds from the centre.
  • Shelldiver (Nov 2025, $4.99): oxygen dives, a big branching tree, about 590k copies (estimated), 98% positive.
  • Digseum: 2 to 4 hours, relics that level up when you find duplicates, 97% positive.
  • A Game About Digging A Hole: 1 million copies. Its twist ending is what people talk about.

What every hit has

  • A short run, then spending in one big tree, then a stronger run.
  • Something new every 20 to 40 minutes: a zone, currency, enemy, tab or machine.
  • A boss that marks the end of each tier and gives a permanent unlock.
  • Automation about a third of the way in.
  • A real ending. Players praise "respects my time" and "clear end goal".

Risks we must design around

  • Shelldiver is close. It is a cozy turtle game. We go the other way: drilling, dread, bosses, a twist.
  • Drill Deep (2026) is a cozy-to-eldritch drilling idle game. Same mood at the end, but no sub, no ocean, no bosses.
  • The name "Deep Line" is taken on Steam by a small horror game. We need a new name.
  • Refunds: Steam refunds anything under 2 hours played. The main path must take 2.5 hours or more.
  • Most-hated flaws: late grind walls, "nothing new after X", unclear upgrades, save loss.

What makes ours stand out

  • A physical drill sub you can see, with every upgrade visible on it.
  • A descent that turns into horror, told over the radio, ending under the trench floor.
  • Darkness and a headlamp: past 200 m you only see what you light.
  • A boss that guards every zone, each with its own trick.
  • A depth scale that explodes: 50 m, then 1 km, then 66 km, then a readout that breaks.
Core loop

One dive, then one decision

1 · LAUNCHThe winch drops you at your deepest checkpoint.
2 · DIVESink, drill ore and creatures, grab air pockets, dodge hazards. Oxygen is the timer.
3 · BOSSReach the zone floor and the guardian rises. Beat it to move the checkpoint.
4 · SURFACESell the haul. Credits, Specimens and Pearls go to the dock.
5 · REFITBuy in the tree, slot relics, read the logbook. Launch again.

Dives last 10 seconds at the start and around 60 to 90 seconds late in the game. Early on you buy something every 30 to 60 seconds, later every few minutes. Something new appears every 20 minutes at most.

Upgrade tree

A real tree, grown from the sub

The whole tree starts as just the Minnow and four parts. Buying a part reveals the parts connected to it, so the tree grows outward across the game. Locked parts show as silhouettes with the exact reason. Every part shows its level, its price and its effect before you buy it. Click or tab through the nodes below to read them.

Three currencies, introduced one at a time

  • Credits from everything you break. Used all game.
  • Specimens from creatures, from zone 2. Spent in Biology.
  • Abyssal Pearls from bosses and pearl oysters, from zone 3. Spent on drill heads, pressure hulls and the Ancient branch.

How the tree screen works

  • Drag to pan, wheel to zoom; the view is remembered between dives.
  • Hover shows now → next, price and what it unlocks. Shift-click buys max.
  • Parts you can afford glow. Milestone parts (drill heads, pressure hulls) are diamonds.
  • Icons and level rings stay readable when zoomed out, including on a Steam Deck.

Relics

24 relics, pulled from wrecks and bosses

Relics sit in a glass case at the dock. Each one gives a passive bonus you feel in the next dive. Finding a duplicate ranks it up from I to III. Six of them are the Heron's survey logs, which tell the story and unlock the second ending.

Story

Told over the radio, then not

Act 1 · The job

Meridian Salvage hires you to strip ore from a trench where a survey sub, the Heron, went missing. The captain talks you down on channel 16. Everything is routine, apart from the size of the things below.

Act 2 · The Heron

You find the Heron's wreck and its pilot's logs. She kept going deeper on purpose. The city walls carry the same warning over and over. The captain stops answering some questions.

Act 3 · The Red Below

The floor splits. The water boils. The radio turns to static, then the captain's voice comes back wrong. The depth readout stops making sense. Meridian was never paying you for ore.

Two endings

Main: you break the Heart and the trench closes over you. True: with all six Heron logs, you learn what the Heart is and can seal it instead, and surface. Credits roll either way; the post-game opens after.

Story is never a text wall. It arrives as radio lines during dives, log pages in the logbook, and changes in the world itself: the lamp flickers, the HUD glitches, the numbers break.

Build order

Four big passes, back to back

Once you approve, I build straight through without stopping for approval between passes. After each pass the game link updates so you can play it. You only need to step in if something feels wrong.

  1. Engine and tree. Rebuild the game around data files for zones, creatures, bosses and the tree. New radial tree screen with pan, zoom, reveal and tooltips. Three currencies, drill heads, pressure hulls. The simulator updated for the full 9-zone curve.
  2. All the content. Nine zones with their own look, about 30 creatures and hazards that behave differently, all nine bosses, relics and wreck caches, the radio script, the logs and both endings.
  3. A finished game around it. Settings, robust saves with backups and export, stats, depth map, 40 achievements, onboarding for the first two minutes, code-generated music and sound for every zone, a text table ready for translation.
  4. Balance and ship. Tune to about 3 hours with the simulator and your playtests. Package with Electron for Windows, Mac, Linux and Steam Deck, wire Steam achievements and cloud saves, cut a 30-minute demo, and render store art and screenshots from the game itself.

This is several long build sessions, not one. Balance is the part that needs you: play it and tell me where it drags.
