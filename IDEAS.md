# Game ideas: short incremental games

## What makes an idea a "10/10" here

A game in this list has to pass all six tests:

1. **Understood from a 5-second clip.** Someone scrolling TikTok or YouTube Shorts gets it without sound or text.
2. **The number keeps going up, and you can see it.** Something visibly grows: bigger, deeper, faster, more.
3. **Done in 1–3 hours.** Short games get finished, and finished games get good reviews.
4. **Cheap art.** Simple shapes or basic pixel art are enough. The satisfying part comes from effects and sound.
5. **Sells at $2.99–$4.99 on Steam**, with a free demo on itch.io, CrazyGames and Steam Next Fest.
6. **Works in portrait orientation** too, so a mobile version later is realistic.

Nobody can guarantee a hit. These ideas have the best odds because proven games already use the same core loops. Every name is a placeholder until we check that it isn't already taken.

## The list

| # | Name | One-line pitch | Why people click | Difficulty (1–5) |
|---|------|----------------|------------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Deepline** | Sink down a vertical shaft, drill rocks, upgrade, reach deeper bosses. | Depth meter plus boss at the bottom is a clear goal. Prototype already exists. | 1 |
| 2 | **Tiny Singularity** | You're a small black hole. Eat dust, then rocks, then planets, then galaxies. | Absurd growth in scale is the most shareable feeling in games (Katamari, Hole.io). | 2 |
| 3 | **Snowball Summit** | Roll a snowball down a mountain. It grows, flattens trees, then houses, then towns. Upgrade and roll again. | Growth you can see, plus destruction. The rest of each run plays itself, so it's easy to watch. | 2 |
| 4 | **Peg Drop** | A Plinko-style incremental: drop balls through pegs, buy multipliers, fill the screen with balls. | Physics chaos is hypnotic in clips. Plinko idle games already perform on mobile. No real-money gambling. | 2 |
| 5 | **Mow the Planet** | Mow grass on a tiny round planet. Upgrade the mower until you're mowing whole worlds. | "Oddly satisfying" cleaning content is a huge audience. Very cheap art. | 2 |
| 6 | **Moth Lamp** | You're a porch lamp at night. Moths fly in and you zap them. Upgrade to a lighthouse, then the sun. | Cozy-spooky mood, simple controls, funny escalation. | 1 |
| 7 | **Ant Empire** | Guide ants to crumbs, grow the colony, raid the picnic, then the whole house. | Ant-colony videos regularly go viral. Swarms look great in motion. | 3 |
| 8 | **Scrap Magnet** | A crane magnet pulls junk from a scrapyard. Sell it and build a giant robot piece by piece. | Physics mess plus a clear "build the robot" goal. | 3 |
| 9 | **Dice Dungeon Rows** | Roll dice to attack rows of monsters marching down. Collect dice with special faces. | Luck-based roguelikes (Balatro, CloverPit) are hot right now. Close to Row Divers. | 4 |
| 10 | **Leaf Storm** | Rake and blow autumn leaves. Leaf piles grow into hurricanes that clear whole cities. | Cozy seasonal theme that's easy to market every autumn. Satisfying particle physics. | 2 |

## My top 3 to test first

1. **Deepline.** It already exists. Play it and decide if the loop is fun.
2. **Tiny Singularity.** It has the strongest hook for short videos.
3. **Peg Drop.** It's the easiest to also port to mobile.

The plan is to build each one as a 2-minute prototype, play them, and keep the one that's hardest to put down.
