# What players praise and complain about in small active / arena / mining incrementals (for "Trenchbore")

Data date: all Steam numbers were pulled on 2026-09-26 from Steam's public review API (`store.steampowered.com/appreviews/<appid>?json=1&language=all&purchase_type=all`) and the store `appdetails` API. Percent positive = total_positive / total_reviews across all languages and purchase types, so it can differ by a point or so from the label on the store page. "Typical playtime" is the **median playtime-at-review of the 100 most recent reviews** (all languages). I used it because HowLongToBeat and TrueSteamAchievements could not be fetched (JS-rendered or 403). Review quotes come from each game's 100 "most helpful" English positive reviews and 100 most helpful English negative reviews. The review links are permanent Steam review URLs.

## For each game: Steam review count, % positive, price, typical playtime, release date

### Takeaway
The best-reviewed games in this niche are cheap ($0-7), short (median about 1-5 hours at review), and finishable. They are Nodebuster (97.4%, 14k reviews, $2.99, about 4.6 h), Digseum (97.7%, 8.2k, $2.99, about 2.8 h), Magic Archery (98.4%, 8.5k, free, about 1.1 h) and Bills Must Be Paid (96.7%, 11k, $6.99, about 4.7 h). The mining/"dig-down" games closest to Trenchbore score noticeably lower (76-93%). Their scores are dragged down by weightless digging, grind in the second half, and zones that do not change.

### Inferences
- There is a clear sweet spot: $1-5, 2-6 hours, a defined ending, and a 95%+ rating is achievable (Nodebuster, Digseum, Bills Must Be Paid). A $6.99 price can still reach 96-97% (Bills Must Be Paid, Rusty's Retirement). Price alone does not sink a game; perceived value per hour does.
- The dig/mining games sit around 80-90%. The complaints in the next sections point to the reasons: feel, second-half grind, and sameness between zones. Trenchbore's theme puts it in the harder bucket unless it fixes those.

### Gaps
- HowLongToBeat numbers were not obtained: the site did not render and TrueSteamAchievements returned 403. The medians of reviewer playtime stand in for them. Real HLTB "main story" figures are probably a bit lower, because reviewers often idle or chase 100%.
- Void Scrappers, Ball x Pit, Mr. Mine and Idle Slayer are not short incrementals. They are included for contrast only.

## Recurring PRAISE in reviews

### Takeaway
Players reward four things:
1. A fast, satisfying early loop.
2. Visible power growth ("number go up", screens full of particles).
3. A big skill/talent tree to fill in.
4. A short, complete experience that is honestly priced.

Many top-voted positive reviews explicitly praise the game for being short and ending.

### Inferences
- Trenchbore's structure (short dives, a radial tree between dives, 9 zones with an ending) matches the praised template. The two praise lines that matter most are "powers you can see and feel as they come online" and "it ends before it drags".
- Every run or zone should add a visible "Oh yeah!" power beat (the "lighting" moment). Players remember these beats more than stat increments.
- Nostalgia for Motherload and flash-era games is a free marketing angle for a drill-and-dig game.

### Gaps
- Few reviews talk about the first 5 minutes as such. The evidence is indirect: "best part was the first 20-30 minutes" and "If you don't like it within the first 5 minutes, it's not going to change your mind" ([Bills Must Be Paid review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198025195084/recommended/4421010/)).

## Recurring COMPLAINTS in reviews

### Takeaway
The dominant complaint is the second-half collapse. The early game is great. Then either (a) the player becomes so strong that input no longer matters ("plays itself"), or (b) costs spike so the end is a grind wall or a single linear node. Close behind are:
- Too short for the price, or an abrupt or anticlimactic ending.
- A linear tree with no real choices ("no builds").
- Low enemy or zone variety.
- Performance and photosensitivity problems.
- Missing quality of life (QoL): skip screens, run in background, toggle-instead-of-hold, clear node states.

### Inferences
- For Trenchbore, the biggest single risk is zones 6-9. Players cannot become "invincible idlers", the last purchases cannot be one linear node, and deep zones cannot be reskins. Each zone needs a new material, enemy behaviour or hazard: "the deeper you go, the more interesting it gets."

## What separates a ~95% game from a ~75-85% game in this niche

### Takeaway
Based on the contrast between the top and bottom of the sample:
- **~95%+ games** are dirt cheap relative to their length, never stall (little waiting, no giant walls), have a clear goal and ending, and tolerate "wish it were longer" as their main complaint.
- **75-85% games** add friction:
  - Confusing or excess resources, or unclear multipliers (Coin Factory, Lyca, Feed the Reactor).
  - A grind or slog in the second half (Piñata, Keep on Mining, Bore Blasters).
  - Low variety (Row Divers).
  - Poor game feel (Super Motherload).
  - Performance problems (To The Core, Lyca, Keep Digging).
  - Price above the perceived hours (holedown, Feed the Reactor).
  - Anti-player monetization or ads (Super Mining Mechs, Idle Slayer).

### Inferences
- A practical recipe for 95%:
  - Price at or below about $1 per hour of median play for a first game ($2.99-4.99 for 3-5 h).
  - Keep every wait under about 30 s.
  - Give every zone a new mechanic.
  - Make the final hour the climax rather than a grind.
  - Keep ending plus post-game (endless dive / "dig out the whole backyard") optional.
  - Ship with working accessibility toggles, no ads, and an active developer in the forums.
- Use accurate tags. Trenchbore should say "short active incremental" or "shortcremental" with an estimated length on the store page, so "too short" reviews are pre-empted.

### Gaps
- The sample cannot separate the effect of YouTuber coverage (e.g., Real Civil Engineer) from design quality in the top games' review counts.

## r/incremental_games users' stated preferences (active vs idle, prestige, pacing, no ads/MTX, clear completion)

### Takeaway
Reddit itself could not be accessed: WebSearch rejects reddit.com, and WebFetch is blocked for both reddit.com and old.reddit.com. Proxy sources from the same community point the same way:
- Strong appetite for short, finite "shortcremental" games with an actual ending.
- Active play with minimal waiting.
- Prestige that changes something, rather than repeating content.
- Hostility to ads, MTX and developers nerfing progress.
- Rising fatigue with shallow "node-likes".

### Cited Findings
- **Actual endings and more interaction.** ResetEra thread (Jan 2025): players value "an actual ending" so it doesn't become "annoying to reach the next monetary goal", and say games "need more interaction, otherwise I can go make lunch and finish the game". — [ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/bite-sized-incremental-games.1087230/)

### Inferences
- For Trenchbore:
  - Mostly active dives with automation/idle unlocks late.
  - Allow the game to keep running when unfocused.
  - Any "prestige" (e.g., a relic-based rebirth) should unlock new behaviour, not just a ×2.
  - No ads or cross-promotion screens, no MTX.
  - State the ending and length clearly.
