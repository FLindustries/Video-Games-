# Sales, Revenue and Market Viability of Small Paid Incremental Games (for "Trenchbore", ~$3 browser incremental)

Research date: 2026-09-26. All Steam review counts, prices and release dates below were pulled live from Steam's public store APIs (`store.steampowered.com/appreviews/{id}?json=1` and `/api/appdetails`) on 2026-09-26 unless marked otherwise. Anything labelled ESTIMATE is derived, not reported.

## 1. What do comparable small incrementals earn on Steam?

### Takeaway
The well-known $3-5 incrementals (Nodebuster, Tower Wizard, Digseum, Keep on Mining!, A Game About Digging A Hole) are genuine hits in the high hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars gross, but they are the survivors. Clones in the same niche can sell a few hundred copies (e.g., "Another Game About Digging A Hole", 6 reviews, around $500-1,000 gross estimated). Even among named, successful comparables, a $2.99 game with ~200 reviews (Row Divers) is roughly a $17k-34k gross game.

### Cited Findings

**Live Steam data (2026-09-26) and Boxleiter ESTIMATES.** Method: total Steam reviews x 30 (low) to x 60 (high) = copies. Gross = copies x current US list price. Net-to-dev ≈ 58.6% of gross, the ratio one tracker reported for Tower Wizard ($879.3K gross → $515.2K net) ([GameRevenueData via search](https://gamerevenuedata.com/games/tower-wizard/)).

## 2. What is the distribution of outcomes for small Steam games?

### Takeaway
The median Steam release earns very little. About two-thirds of 2025 releases grossed under $1,000 lifetime, about 40% did not reach $100, and only about 8% grossed over $100k. Revenue is extremely concentrated.

### Cited Findings
- About 20,000 games were released on Steam in 2025 (55+ per day). 65.9% earned under $1,000, ~40% had not reached $100, only 8% grossed >$100,000, and ~300 crossed $1M. These are Gamalytic-derived estimates, "not audited sales figures from Valve" ([game-developers.org](https://game-developers.org/2025-steam-game-revenue-distribution)).
- 47.4% of 2025 releases are estimated to have sold under 100 copies, and 28% sold 100-1,000 copies ([Steam Page Analyzer summary](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-review-to-sales-multiplier)).
- The median indie game earned roughly $249 gross in 2025, about $174 after Valve's cut. More than 5,000 games earned under $100 in the year (search summary of [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/12/22/indie-projects-generated-a-quarter-of-the-total-game-revenue-on-steam-by-the-end-of-2025-analytics) / [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-49645)). A different site claims a $5k-15k lifetime median ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-revenue-data)). That figure appears to cover only "commercial-intent" games, so the two sources conflict because they measure different populations.
- Indie games made about $4.4-4.5B of Steam's ~$17.7B 2025 revenue (~25%). The top 5 indie releases took $500M+ ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/12/22/indie-projects-generated-a-quarter-of-the-total-game-revenue-on-steam-by-the-end-of-2025-analytics); [game-developers.org](https://game-developers.org/2025-steam-game-revenue-distribution)).
- Across 53,597 Steam releases from mid-2023 to mid-2026, the top 1% of titles captured ~94% of estimated revenue (Sulka Haro census, reported by [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study)).

### Inferences
- The realistic base rate for a first game from a solo non-developer with no audience is the median, which means hundreds of dollars, not thousands. Being a polished, finished 3-5 hour game with a clear hook moves the odds up. The distribution is still winner-take-most.

### Gaps
- No incremental-genre-specific median was found. The WN Hub / AppMagic idle-games report (May 2026) with median revenue and retention is paywalled ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-50959)).

## 3. itch.io: do paid (browser) games make money, and how does itch compare with free web portals?

### Takeaway
itch.io is a good testing ground and a poor revenue source. Chris Zukowski's benchmark says flatly: "You will not make money from Itch.io. Nobody does." Browser play strongly boosts plays, and paid HTML5 incrementals do exist on itch (Tower Wizard at $2.99+ has 781 ratings). The median itch page, though, gets ~1,600 lifetime views. Ad-share portals pay more per player at scale, but only at hundreds of thousands of plays.

### Cited Findings
- **Revenue share and fees:** itch's default cut is 10%, and the seller can set it from 0-100%. PayPal and Stripe charge $0.30 + 2.9% per transaction. itch recommends a minimum price of $2 because of fixed fees. Payouts need a $5 balance, take 7-14+ days, and require tax verification. itch acts as merchant of record and handles VAT ([itch.io docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments)).
- **Traffic benchmark** (169 devs, How To Market A Game, May 2025): lifetime page views have a median of 1,582, a 30th percentile of ~398, a 70th percentile of 12,150, and a mean of 54,483 (skewed by a few hits). Downloads have a median of 113. Browser games get ~3x more players, with browser plays at 27-44% of views versus 6-7% for download-only games. Zukowski's advice is to use itch as a 1-2 month test for whether a game has "the magic", then scale up on Steam (examples: The Roottrees are Dead, Peglin) ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/12/benchmark-itch-io-traffic/)).
- A secondary guide claims "having no demo on itch, and only a paid game is almost a guarantee for having no commercial success". It also says donation rates on free games are 1-3% of downloads and typical itch prices are $5-15 ([Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/tutorials/how-to-make-money-on-itchio-indie-game-guide)). This is a secondary source.
- A secondary source claims "~80% of paid games earn under $50/month; most earn under $100 lifetime" on itch ([Cinevva web monetization guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-monetization)). The primary data source was not given, so treat it as unverified.
- **A real paid browser incremental on itch:** Tower Wizard (Barribob) sells for "$2.99 USD or more", is playable in the browser, and has 4.6/5 from 781 ratings. The developer announced the Steam move in Dec 2024 and released there in mid-2025 ([itch.io page](https://barribob.itch.io/tower-wizard)). On Steam it then earned an estimated $0.5-0.9M gross (see Q1).
- **Creator disclosure example:** one developer reported $2,461.01 gross from 1,910 payments across their whole itch portfolio over years ([Nathalie Lawhead, Candybox blog](https://www.nathalielawhead.com/candybox/my-gross-revenue-on-itch-io-transparently-sharing-all-my-stats-earnings-and-speaking-on-how-supportive-of-a-base-itch-io-has)).
- **itch audience size:** estimates range from ~44.5M monthly visitors (Exploding Topics, Jul 2026) to 62.6M visits (Xranks) to 112M visits (Semrush, Oct 2025). The audience is 73% male, the largest age group is 18-24, and top countries are the US, Brazil and Russia. The largest traffic source is organic search at ~41% ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/itch.io/); [Exploding Topics](https://analytics.explodingtopics.com/website/itch.io); [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/itch.io/overview/)).
- **Web portals (ad revenue share):** Poki uses a 50/50 split on portal traffic. CrazyGames gives 60% of ad revenue and 70% of in-game purchases (2026 jam terms). Rewarded-video eCPM is $15-28 in the US, $8-15 in the EU, and $1-3 in tier-3 markets. A "well-performing casual game" makes $200-2,000/month, and the top Poki studios make up to €1M/yr. One developer's 8 WebGL games earned €556.92 from 451,327 CrazyGames plays, about €1.20 per 1,000 plays (2018 data). Non-exclusive licences sell for $300-800 per portal ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-monetization); [CrazyGames dev portal](https://developer.crazygames.com/)). CrazyGames pays monthly via Tipalti above a €100 minimum ([Cinevva CrazyGames guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-crazygames)).
- One indie developer reported 67M gameplays across 5 Poki games, reaching ~200k plays/day, which became primary income (reported in [Cinevva / search summary](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-monetization)).

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE, $3 itch sale economics:** $3.00 minus the processor fee ($0.30 + 2.9% ≈ $0.39) minus itch's 10% (≈ $0.30) leaves ≈ $2.31 to the seller, about 77%. On Steam a $3 sale gives the dev 70% before VAT, refunds and regional pricing, and ~59% of list is a realistic net (Tower Wizard ratio).
- **ESTIMATE, median-case itch funnel for Trenchbore:** 1,582 views x ~35% browser-play rate ≈ 550 demo players. If 1-3% buy the $3 full game, that is ~5-17 sales, or roughly $12-40 net. Even at the 70th percentile of views (12,150), the same rates give ~40-130 sales, or ~$100-300 net. This is an inference from the benchmark plus the secondary 1-3% conversion heuristic, not measured data.
- Splitting into a free demo page plus a paid page a week later means the paid page starts from zero views. It may be better to have one page with a free demo and the paid full game on the same page, the way Tower Wizard does "$2.99 or more" with browser play.
- A free ad-supported version on CrazyGames or Poki pays around €1-2 per 1,000 plays. That only beats itch sales if the game reaches roughly 100k+ plays, and a 3-5 hour finite game has less repeat-play ad inventory than an endless idle game. Portals also expect their own SDK integration (ads, saves), which is extra engineering for an AI-built project.

### Gaps
- I found no primary, aggregate itch.io revenue data (itch does not publish medians).
- I found no postmortem of a paid-only HTML5 incremental on itch with sales numbers. Tower Wizard's itch sales are not public.
- Newgrounds, Armor Games and Kongregate current revenue-share terms were not researched or verified.

## 4. Steam costs, wishlists, Next Fest and demos

### Takeaway
Steam costs $100 per game, recouped once the game passes $1,000 adjusted gross. It requires a 21-day wait after paying and a public "Coming Soon" page for at least 2 weeks. First-week sales are typically ~10-25% of launch wishlists, and the median Next Fest game gains only ~800-1,100 wishlists.

### Cited Findings
- Steam Direct fee: $100 per app, non-refundable, recouped after the product reaches $1,000 Adjusted Gross Revenue ([Steamworks docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee)).
- There is "a 21-day waiting period between when you paid the app fee and when you can release". A public "coming soon" page is required "for at least two weeks" ([Steamworks onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding)).
- About 40% of 2025 releases never reached $100 revenue ([game-developers.org](https://game-developers.org/2025-steam-game-revenue-distribution)). Since the fee is only recouped after $1,000, 65.9% of games never recoup it.
- Wishlist conversion (GameDiscoverCo, Oct 17 2025): for games with 25k+ launch wishlists, the median first-week sales / launch wishlists ratio is 0.15x. It is 0.14x for non-adult games and 0.10x for games over $10. The range "varies by 10-20x, not 10-20%" ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)).
- A secondary source cites median conversion of ~15% for under 5k wishlists and 20% for 5k-40k ([search summary](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-wishlists-before-launch)).
- Zukowski's tallies of median Next Fest wishlists: 884 (Jun 2024), 916 (Oct 2024), 1,079 (Feb 2025), 806 (Feb 2026). Games that launched their demo months before the fest got about 2.5x the median wishlists of games that shadow-dropped during the fest (via [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/do-steam-demos-increase-sales); original: [How To Market A Game benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/)).
- Wishlist-to-buyer data for Next Fest demo games: [Alinea Analytics](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/wishlist-to-buyer-conversions-for). Not read in detail.

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** a median Next Fest showing (~800-1,000 wishlists) plus prior itch traffic might produce ~1,000-2,000 launch wishlists. At 0.15-0.2x that is ~150-400 first-week sales. At $2.99 that is roughly $450-1,200 gross in week one, or about $270-700 net. Long-tail lifetime sales are often a multiple of week one. The $100 fee is small against that.
- Steam's discoverability (wishlists, Next Fest, Discovery Queue, "More like this" next to Nodebuster and Digseum) is the reason the comparables in Q1 made money. Staying itch-only gives up this channel.

### Gaps
- No incremental-specific wishlist conversion data was found.

## 5. AI-generated content: Steam and itch.io policy, and player sentiment

### Takeaway
Both stores allow AI-made games but ask for disclosure. Steam's Jan 2026 form covers AI content "consumed by players" (art, sound, text, store assets) and exempts dev-efficiency tools such as code assistants. itch's disclosure includes "AI Generated Code" and is optional for games. Tagged AI games can be filtered out by users. A meaningful minority of players are hostile, and AI-disclosed games on Steam underperform their share of releases.

### Cited Findings
- **itch.io (Nov 20 2024):** a "Generative AI disclosure" field was added. It is mandatory for asset creators and optional for games. Categories are Graphics, Sound, Text & Dialog, and Code. "Yes" adds an "AI Generated" tag plus subtags (e.g., "AI Generated Code"), and "No" adds a "No AI" tag. Untagged AI assets become ineligible for browse indexing. The admin said LLM-generated code (e.g., Copilot output) counts as generative AI content, with devs to "use best judgment" ([itch.io developer update](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/itchio-marketplace-now-requires-asset-creators-to-disclose-their-use-of-generative-ai-130031999.html)). Users can filter browse results by these tags ([GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/itchio-now-requires-gen-ai-content-to-be-tagged-in-all-games-and-asset-packs-711e824843d410eab51fcd9436836294/)). Some users complained that itch later made the AI labels less visible ([itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/4527500/im-dismayed-that-itchio-decided-to-hide-which-gamesassets-are-ai-and-which-arent)).
- **Steam (updated Jan 2026):** the disclosure form now says "efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section" and is concerned with AI-generated content consumed by players. It splits AI use into pre-generated and live-generated content. Disclosure is still required for in-game art, sound, narrative, localization and store and marketing assets ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/); [TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/345302/steam-ai-disclosure-gets-clarification-for-ai-in-dev-tools)).
- **Share of Steam releases with AI disclosure:** 10.9% (2024), 19.9% (2025), 30.8% (2026 to mid-year). AI-flagged launches grew from ~13 to ~530 a month and account for 60-90% of release-count growth. AI-disclosed games hold 10-27% of estimated sales, up from 3-6% in 2024, which is below their share of supply. Among AI games that failed, 72% used AI for visuals, while successful ones leaned on AI for voice (24%) and localization (18%). "AI made shipping a game dramatically cheaper. It did nothing to make anyone want to play it." (Sulka Haro census, via [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study)).
- AI-disclosed games have grossed an estimated $660M on Steam ([Totally Human Media](https://www.totallyhuman.io/blog/games-with-ai-disclosures-have-grossed-an-estimated-660m-on-steam)).
- **Player sentiment** (Steam-focused survey, found through search results that did not name the pollster clearly; primary source not verified): ~43% have no major issue buying AI-disclosed games, 26% are neutral, ~31% view AI use negatively, and 8% would refuse to buy. ~44% read disclosures carefully ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026/) / search summary).

### Inferences
- Trenchbore's art is procedural (drawn by AI-written code) and its sound is synthesized. That is arguably not "AI-generated assets" in the image-generator sense, but the code is entirely LLM-written. On itch, by the admin's own guidance, the honest choice is to tick AI disclosure with the "Code" subtag. On Steam after Jan 2026, AI-written code arguably falls under the exempt "efficiency tools", but art and sound produced by AI-authored code is a grey area. Disclosing is the lower-risk path. Being found out undisclosed carries more reputational risk than the disclosure itself.
- The data suggests visible AI imagery is what players punish. Code-drawn vector-style art, if it looks deliberate, is less likely to get the "AI slop" reaction than image-generator art. "Solo creator made this with an AI coding assistant" is likely to draw some hostility in r/incremental_games and itch comments, and should be expected.

### Gaps
- Primary source for the 31%/8% player survey was not verified.
- itch.io's 2025-2026 changes to how prominently AI tags are displayed and filtered were not fully verified.

## 6. Is the "short/active incremental" niche growing or saturated in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
It is both growing and crowding fast. Idle and incremental games on Steam increased roughly fivefold in two years, and Nodebuster explicitly spawned a wave of look-alikes. Demand is real (several $3 hits in 2024-2025), but supply is rising faster, much of it cheap and AI-assisted.

### Cited Findings
- "Over two years, the number of idle games on Steam has increased fivefold" (WN Hub exclusive, May 27 2026. The full data is paywalled) ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-50959)).
- Steam has 2,361 games tagged incremental. Most idle titles are priced $3-15. IdleOn (F2P) made $22M+ in 2025. Tower Wizard sold ~555K copies and Cookie Clicker ~720K on Steam ([AppMagic, via search](https://appmagic.rocks/research/idle-steam-games-2026); [Steam 250 incremental tag](https://steam250.com/tag/incremental)).
- Laura Michet on Nodebuster: "a platter of small, well-illustrated mechanics that other games could steal". It "exists in order to spawn a genre of coattail-riders" ([Laura Michet's blog](https://blog.lauramichet.com/played-nodebuster/)). Digseum followed with very similar UI and game feel, applied to digging and relics ([search summary of the Mancunion/Steam related pages](https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game/)).
- itch.io has a "nodebuster" tag with explicit clones, e.g., Shapebuster ("a simple Nodebuster clone made for educational purposes") and PACHINCRO! ("inspired by Nodebuster") ([itch.io tag-nodebuster](https://itch.io/games/tag-nodebuster); [Shapebuster](https://frajdagames.itch.io/shapebuster)).
- Digging and drilling incrementals specifically: Digseum (Dec 2024), A Game About Digging A Hole (Feb 2025), Keep on Mining! (Jul 2025), Drill Core (Jul 2025), BORE BLASTERS (2024), Dig Too Deep (2026), and the clone Another Game About Digging A Hole (Apr 2025, 6 reviews). All data is from the Steam API (Q1).
- AI-flagged games are now ~31% of 2026 Steam releases and drive most release growth ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study)).

### Inferences
- Trenchbore sits where two crowded sub-niches overlap: the short "Nodebuster-like" active incremental and the dig/drill-down theme. Its differentiators are the submarine and drill arm, 9 zones descending to hell, bosses, relics and a radial skill tree. On paper those match what the hits share: short 3-5 hour runs, a strong fantasy, satisfying numbers. They also match what dozens of clones already offer. Execution and a hook that is readable at a glance (trailer, GIF, capsule) will matter more than the feature list.
- The pattern among clones is clear: copying a hit's look gets near-zero sales (Another Game About Digging A Hole). The hits had a distinct visual identity or a clear twist.

## 7. How do these games get found?

### Takeaway
Hard evidence is thin. The pattern visible in the sources is that successful small incrementals get found on Steam (algorithm, "more like this", streamers and YouTubers covering cheap viral games), and itch serves as the testing ground. Specific attribution of any comparable's success to named creators (Splattercat, Olexa, Wanderbots) could not be sourced.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube coverage exists for Nodebuster, e.g., "Nodebuster, An Experimental Minimalist Incremental Game" and "This God Virus is Insane in Nodebuster" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KsM950rUxsA); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tdT5T4jkdtY)). It also has a Twitch category ([Twitch](https://www.twitch.tv/directory/category/nodebuster)).
- A Game About Digging A Hole "went viral on social media" and sold 1M+ copies ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/a-game-about-digging-a-hole/one-million-sales)).
- Steam's "More like this" for Nodebuster surfaces similar incrementals ([Steam recommended](https://store.steampowered.com/recommended/morelike/app/3107330/)). Steam's recommendation surface is a discovery channel for look-alikes.
- itch's organic search is ~41% of its traffic, followed by direct and organic social ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/itch.io/)). itch serves as a 1-2 month "does it have the magic" test ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/12/benchmark-itch-io-traffic/)).
- Tower Wizard's path: paid browser game on itch, with the Steam move announced Dec 2024 and launched on Steam mid-2025, reaching hundreds of thousands of copies ([itch.io](https://barribob.itch.io/tower-wizard); Q1 data).

### Inferences
- For a zero-audience creator, the realistic free channels are r/incremental_games (which has a strong culture of posting web and itch incrementals), itch browse/new, short gameplay clips on TikTok, YouTube Shorts or Reddit, and emailing incremental-focused YouTubers and streamers with a key. The discovery flywheel that paid the comparables mostly ran through Steam.

## 8. Overall viability assessment for Trenchbore (~$3 paid browser incremental, itch-first, no Steam "for now")

### Takeaway
As planned (itch.io only, $3, free demo then paid build a week later), the likely outcome is tens to low hundreds of dollars, not thousands. That is consistent with itch benchmarks and Zukowski's "nobody makes money on itch". The genre itself can clearly make money at $3: Nodebuster, Tower Wizard and Digseum are each ESTIMATED at $0.7M+ gross on Steam. So the plan's biggest money lever is not price but platform: itch as the test, then Steam (a $100 fee) if the itch test shows traction.

### Cited Findings
- Median itch page has 1,582 lifetime views; browser plays are 27-44% of views; "You will not make money from Itch.io" ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/12/benchmark-itch-io-traffic/)).
- 65.9% of 2025 Steam releases grossed under $1,000 ([game-developers.org](https://game-developers.org/2025-steam-game-revenue-distribution)).
- Tower Wizard moved from paid itch browser game ($2.99+, 781 ratings) to Steam and reached an ESTIMATED $0.5-0.9M gross ([itch.io](https://barribob.itch.io/tower-wizard); [GameRevenueData](https://gamerevenuedata.com/games/tower-wizard/)).
- Steam entry costs $100, recouped after $1,000, with a 21-day wait and a 2-week Coming Soon page ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding)).

### Inferences (scenario ESTIMATES, not forecasts)
- **itch-only, median traction:** ~500 browser demo players leading to ~5-20 sales at $3, about $10-50 net.
- **itch-only, strong traction** (70th-90th percentile views, a front-page r/incremental_games post): ~5k-20k plays leading to ~50-400 sales, about $100-900 net.
- **Steam add-on, modest** (e.g., 150-400 reviews like Row Divers or Void Scrappers): ~5k-20k copies, about $15k-60k gross and $9k-35k net. That is plausible only if itch shows real demand (for example, thousands of plays, high ratings, people asking for Steam).
- **Steam hit** (Nodebuster, Tower Wizard or Digseum tier): 250k+ copies, about $0.7M+ gross. This is rare; the top ~1% capture ~94% of revenue.
- **Price:** $3 is in line with the genre's hits ($2.99 is the modal price for Nodebuster, Tower Wizard, Digseum and Row Divers). On itch, "$2.99 or more" (pay-what-you-want minimum) lets fans overpay. Price is not the bottleneck; traffic is.
- **Structure risk:** a separate demo page plus a separate paid page a week later splits the already-small itch traffic. A single page with a playable browser demo and a buy button for the full version, or a Steam page wishlist link, keeps the funnel intact.
- **AI risk:** disclose honestly (itch "AI Generated Code"; Steam if art or sound counts as AI content). Avoid anything that looks like image-generator art. Expect some community pushback.
- **Honest bottom line:** the build cost is near zero, so any sales are profit and the downside is time. Trenchbore is unlikely to "make money" in a meaningful sense on itch alone. It has a plausible, though not probable, shot at hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars if it proves fun on itch and then goes to Steam with a Coming Soon page, a demo, and Next Fest.

### Gaps
- No primary itch revenue distribution data exists publicly.
- No incremental-specific median revenue was found (the AppMagic/WN Hub report is paywalled).
- Conversion from free itch demo to paid itch purchase is not measured anywhere I could find. The 1-3% used above is a secondary heuristic for donations, applied by analogy.
